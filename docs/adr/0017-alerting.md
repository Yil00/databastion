# ADR-0017: Alerting: channels, signatures, delivery and outbound address policy

- **Status**: Accepted
- **Date**: 2026-09-28
- **Context references**: P3-C, #49 (`console/README.md` "Alerting", `console/src/server/channels.ts`, `channel-secrets.ts`, `notifications.ts`, `net-guard.ts`, `system-alerts.ts`, `alerting-config.ts`, `senders/`, migrations `0017` to `0020`); answers the P3-C questions left open by [ADR-0014](0014-policy-and-incident-model.md)

## Context
Phase 3 sends notifications: e-mail and webhooks for new incidents, plus console alerts that no policy raises (silent agents, agent-integrity events). This is the first time the console connects out to hosts chosen by a user, and the first time it holds third-party secrets (SMTP passwords, webhook tokens). The choices below are hard to change once receivers verify signatures and channels exist in deployed databases:

- how a receiver authenticates a webhook and rejects a replay;
- where channel secrets come from and how they are stored;
- what "sent" means when the worker crashes or a job is repeated;
- what happens when a policy names a channel that does not exist (ADR-0014 left it to P3-C);
- where silent-agent alerts live, given that incidents are what policies raise (ADR-0014);
- how an administrator's URL cannot turn the worker into a proxy towards internal services (SSRF).

## Decision
1. **Webhook signature.** Each request carries `X-DataBastion-Signature: t=<unix seconds>,v1=<hex>`, where `v1 = HMAC-SHA256(signing secret, "<t>.<raw body>")` and `t` is the time of the attempt. Receivers recompute the MAC, compare in constant time and reject a `t` older than **5 minutes** (replay window). `X-DataBastion-Delivery` is stable across retries and is the receiver's deduplication key. `verifyWebhookSignature` in `console/src/server/senders/webhook.ts` is the reference. The `v1=` prefix leaves room for another scheme without breaking receivers.
2. **Channel secrets.**
   - The webhook signing secret is **generated by the console** (256 bits, `whsec_…`), shown once on creation or rotation, never returned again. SMTP passwords are entered by the administrator.
   - Secrets are stored with AES-256-GCM under the HKDF-SHA256 subkey **`notification-channels.v1`** of `DATABASTION_ENCRYPTION_KEY`, with a random 96-bit nonce and AAD = label ‖ format version ‖ channel id ‖ type, so a ciphertext copied to another channel, or relabelled as another type, does not decrypt.
   - The **full webhook URL is encrypted**, in the same record as the signing secret; only its origin is stored in clear. Many webhook URLs embed a token in their path or query.
   - Secrets are never returned by the API, never logged and never written to the audit log. Moving an e-mail channel with a stored password to another host, port, TLS mode or user requires the password again.
3. **At-least-once delivery through a durable outbox.** A delivery row (`notification_deliveries`) is written in the same transaction as the incident or the alert, one per (subject, event, channel) under a unique idempotency key, so repeated or concurrent evaluations never notify twice. The pg-boss queue `notifications.deliver` carries no payload and only wakes the worker (as `policies.evaluate` in ADR-0014); rows are claimed with a lease, sent outside any transaction, retried with backoff and recorded with a closed error code (never a server response, host or URL). A crash between sending and recording sends again with the same delivery id: receivers must deduplicate.
4. **Unknown or disabled channel: skip, not refuse.** A `notify` action naming an unknown or disabled slug does not block the incident: the incident is created and the delivery for that slug is recorded as `skipped` (`unknown_channel` / `channel_disabled`), visible on the incident. The policy API keeps accepting such slugs (policies may be written before their channels); the policy page and form warn about them. A channel created later does not receive past incidents.
5. **Silent-agent and integrity alerts are security events, not incidents.** An agent that was online and has sent no heartbeat for `DATABASTION_SILENT_AGENT_INTERVALS` × 30 s (default 5 minutes) raises one alert per silence episode, recorded as a `security_events` row (`agent.silent`) and an audit entry, and notified to the channels flagged **`system_alerts`**. Agent-integrity events already recorded in `security_events` (rejected batches, `batch_conflict`, foreign targets, `rotation_conflict`) are notified to the same channels, at most once per agent, kind and hour per channel. `security_events` is insert-only for the runtime role, so a compromised console process cannot erase these alerts; `incidents` stay what policies raise (ADR-0014).
6. **One development flag, and a second acknowledgement in production.** `DATABASTION_ALERTING_INSECURE_DEV=1` allows `http://` webhooks, webhooks to internal addresses and plain-text SMTP to a non-loopback relay. Link-local and metadata addresses stay refused. In production the web and worker processes refuse to start when it is set, unless `DATABASTION_ALERTING_INSECURE_DEV_I_UNDERSTAND=1` is also set (then a warning is logged).
7. **Built-in SMTP client.** The worker uses a minimal SMTP client written for this purpose (RFC 5321 subset, no dependency): implicit TLS or mandatory STARTTLS (never a silent downgrade, any byte received in clear after the STARTTLS `220` aborts), certificate verified against the configured host, AUTH PLAIN / LOGIN only over TLS, plain-text UTF-8 messages, bounded replies and timeouts. Reasons: the subset needed is small; the client must connect only to the addresses vetted by the address policy (decision 8) and apply the STARTTLS and reply bounds above, which a general-purpose library would have to be configured and verified to do on every path; and it avoids a dependency in the process that holds the channel secrets.
8. **SSRF model.** Only the **worker** opens outbound connections (webhooks, SMTP); the web process only validates settings and queues tests. For each attempt, the host is resolved and **every** resolved address is checked; one refused address refuses the destination. The socket connects only to the checked addresses (no second resolution, so no DNS rebinding), trying them in the resolver's order with a fallback to the next one on a connection error. IPv4 addresses **embedded** in IPv6 transition addresses (IPv4-mapped, IPv4-translated, IPv4-compatible, NAT64 `64:ff9b::/96`, 6to4, Teredo) are extracted and the stricter class applies. Classes: link-local (including `169.254.169.254` and `fd00:ec2::254`), unspecified, multicast, broadcast and reserved are always refused; loopback, private, CGNAT, ULA, documentation and benchmarking ranges and the transition prefixes are refused for webhooks unless the dev flag is set, and allowed for SMTP (mail relays are usually internal). TLS is always verified against the host name; redirects are never followed; no HTTP proxy is used.
9. **Volume bounds.**
   - **Per-channel hourly budget and digest**: at most `DATABASTION_NOTIFY_MAX_PER_HOUR` (default 30) incident notifications per channel and clock hour (a soft limit). Beyond it, deliveries are recorded as `skipped` (`rate_limited`), and one `notifications.suppressed` digest per channel and hour reports the count. System alerts, tests and digests are not counted.
   - **Test sends**: at most 10 per administrator and 3 per channel per 10 minutes (`429`). These limiters are in memory, per process.

## Consequences
- Webhook receivers must implement the signature check, the 5-minute window and deduplication on `X-DataBastion-Delivery`. Changing the scheme means a new `v` key, not a change of `v1`.
- Changing `DATABASTION_ENCRYPTION_KEY` makes stored channel secrets unusable: deliveries are retried as `secret_unavailable` until the administrator re-enters them.
- A policy can silently notify nobody if its slugs do not match a channel; the `skipped` records and the policy page warnings are the control.
- An outage of the web process alone (worker up) makes every agent silent after the threshold. At worker start, no silence alert is raised during the first threshold, so a console outage does not become one alert per agent.
- The console now has outbound network access from the worker. Deployments that restrict egress must allow the webhook and SMTP destinations from the worker only.

### Residual risks
- **Other NAT64 prefixes.** Only the well-known prefix `64:ff9b::/96` has its embedded IPv4 address extracted. The local-use prefix `64:ff9b:1::/48` is classified as internal as a whole (so allowed for SMTP, even when it embeds a metadata address), and a network-specific prefix chosen by an operator is not known at all (classified by the IPv6 address alone).
- **Silent-agent alerts have no budget.** They are bounded by one alert per agent and silence episode, not by the per-channel hourly budget; a large fleet going silent at once (e.g. a network outage between agents and console) sends one alert per agent.
- **Test-send limits are per process.** Several web replicas would each apply them; the MVP runs one web process.
- **No HTML e-mail.** Messages are plain text only.
- **At-least-once, not exactly once.** Duplicates are possible after a worker crash; they carry the same delivery id and `Message-ID`.

## Rejected alternatives
- **Signature without a timestamp** (MAC over the body only): a captured request can be replayed forever.
- **Secrets chosen by the administrator for webhooks**: human-chosen secrets are weak and reused; the console generates 256 bits instead.
- **Storing the webhook URL in clear**: many URLs carry a bearer token in their path or query.
- **Sending from the evaluation transaction, or carrying the notification in the job payload**: a crash or a lost job loses or duplicates the notification with nothing to reconcile against. The outbox is the record.
- **Refusing a policy or an incident when a slug is unknown**: couples incident creation to notification configuration, and a typo would lose an incident.
- **Silent-agent alerts as incidents**: incidents carry a policy snapshot, a dedup key and runtime-role column grants built for findings (ADR-0014), and an agent-health signal must not be erasable by the runtime role.
- **A single dev flag without a production acknowledgement**: one leftover variable would open SSRF towards internal services in production.
- **Checking only the first resolved address, or resolving again at connection time**: mixed records and DNS rebinding bypass the check.
