# ADR-0021: Access-event correlation: storage and retention, score, baselines, dedup, cap and draining

- **Status**: Accepted
- **Date**: 2026-09-29
- **Refines**: [ADR-0014](0014-policy-and-incident-model.md) (which stays Accepted), decision 3 for the `access_event` source; also records the `access_event` keys announced in decision 1, and corrects its matcher wording (see "Refines ADR-0014")
- **Context references**: P4-C, #54 (`console/README.md` "Audit correlation", `console/src/lib/event-model.ts`, `console/src/server/events.ts`, `console/src/server/event-engine.ts`, `console/src/server/audit-config.ts`, migrations `0021_p4c_access_events.sql` to `0026_p4c_evict_by_cap.sql`)

## Context
Phase 4 adds access events as the second source of incidents. Events differ from findings: they are occurrences, not state; they arrive in bursts (a `pg_dump` sends one event per table); an agent can send many of them, and a hostile agent can choose account names, client addresses and volumes. The console must turn them into few, meaningful incidents, keep what agents reported out of the reach of a compromised console process, bound its own storage and work, and hold no sampled value (I2, [ADR-0007](0007-mask-access-events.md)). [ADR-0014](0014-policy-and-incident-model.md) decision 3 ("resolved means remediated", reopen when a later scan still sees the finding) does not fit a source that has no "later scan". The choices below are hard to change once incidents, baselines and webhook receivers exist.

## Decision
1. **Storage.** `access_events` holds exactly the contract `AccessEvent` fields (ADR-0007: no query text, bound parameter or returned value; unknown fields are rejected before storage), a `principal_key` (SHA-256 of the account name or of its fingerprint, never agent text) and the worker's evaluation. The runtime database role can insert events and update only the evaluation columns (`evaluated_at`, `sensitivity`, `score`, `anomaly`, `baseline_rows`); it cannot rewrite what the agent reported or delete it. `events_batches` (replay records) and `incident_events` (incident ↔ event links) are insert and select only. `principal_baselines` and `audit_configs` are updated in place, never deleted by the runtime role (migration `0022`).
2. **Retention.** `events.purge` (worker, hourly and at start) deletes events whose `ts` is older than `DATABASTION_EVENTS_RETENTION_DAYS` (default **90**, accepted 7 to 3650, other values fall back to the default), 10 000 at a time within a 50 s budget. The runtime role can only do it through `databastion_purge_access_events`, a `SECURITY DEFINER` function owned by the migration role, with a pinned `search_path` (`pg_catalog, pg_temp`), `EXECUTE` revoked from `PUBLIC` and granted to the runtime role only, and its parameters clamped (retention to [7, 3650] days, chunk to [1, 100 000] rows). It never deletes an event not evaluated yet. It deletes `events_batches` 30 days after the event retention, so a late replay from a spool is still recognized as a duplicate, and baselines not updated for longer than the retention. A compromised console process can therefore at worst purge evaluated events older than **7 days**. Incidents keep their counts after their events are purged (migrations `0022`, `0024`).
3. **Score.** Sensitivity of an object = Σ over the classifiers found on it (any column, false positives excluded) of weight × highest confidence, capped at 30. An event's sensitivity is that of its most sensitive object; an event object without schema matches the findings of any schema. `score = sensitivity × log10(1 + rows)`, 0 when the source reports no `rows` or no object is sensitive; for a pre-aggregated event, `rows` is the total. Weights:

   | Classifier | Weight | Classifier | Weight |
   |------------|--------|------------|--------|
   | `secret.aws_key` | 10 | `pii.birth_date` | 3 |
   | `secret.password_hash` | 8 | `pii.email` | 3 |
   | `pii.card_number` | 8 | `pii.phone` | 3 |
   | `pii.iban` | 7 | `pii.postal_address` | 3 |
   | `pii.nir` | 7 | `pii.person_name` | 2 |

   Unlisted ids: 8 for `secret.*`, 2 for `pii.*`, 1 otherwise. The logarithm makes volume count in orders of magnitude and sensitivity linearly.
4. **Baselines.** One per (agent, target, principal), fed only by events that report `rows` and are neither `connect` nor `auth_failure` (so failed logins with random account names create none). Exponentially weighted mean and variance of `ln(1 + rows)` with weight `max(0.05, 1/n)` (the plain mean for the first 20 events, then a memory of about 20 events); warm after **20** events. A warm baseline flags an event as an **anomaly** when `rows >= 1000` and `ln(1 + rows) > mean + max(ln 10, 3 sd)`. The verdict uses the baseline before the event; the event then updates it with its value **capped at that threshold**, so one dump does not raise the baseline while a lasting change is learnt gradually. At most `DATABASTION_BASELINES_PER_TARGET` baselines per target (default 10 000, 10 to 1 000 000): past it, the worker calls `databastion_evict_principal_baselines`, a `SECURITY DEFINER` function (same hardening as decision 2) that takes the cap, clamps it to at least 10 and deletes only the rows beyond it, least recently updated first, at most 1000 per call (migration `0026`). The console's verdict is the `anomaly` policy condition, not a signal.
5. **Conditions and exceptions.** Source `access_event` keys: `signals` (contract ids or `signature.*`, `shape.*`, `volume.*`), `event_actions`, `sources`, `agent_ids`, `target_ids`, `engines`, `principals` / `exclude_principals` (globs on the account name or on the fingerprint sent in its place), `objects` (`database` / `schema` / `object` globs), `min_rows`, `min_score`, `min_sensitivity`, `anomaly`. At least one selective key is required, so a policy never turns every access into an incident. Exceptions apply by agent, target and location (globs that cover every retained object); **a classifier-scoped exception never covers an event**. A policy applies to the events evaluated after its creation or change; **past events are never re-evaluated**.
6. **Dedup scope.** `dedup_key = policy | agent | target | principal | database | UTC hour of the event ts`, where the database is a SHA-256 of the name of the most sensitive retained object (`-` without object). For an unknown account (fingerprint sent instead of the name) and for every `auth_failure`, the principal part is `unknown:<SHA-256 of the client network>`: IPv4 /24, IPv6 /64, IPv4-mapped IPv6 as its IPv4 /24, `local` kept. Random account names or rotating addresses within a network therefore count as one principal. Key parts are identifiers and hashes, never agent text. While the incident of a key is open or acknowledged, later events of the key are added to it (count, total rows, highest score, signals, anomaly, link). Once it is a false positive (administrator decision on the incident: events have no finding to carry it), the rest of that hour is only linked to it. A pre-aggregated or per-table dump thus raises one incident per policy, principal, database and hour.
7. **Resolved: a new incident only when worse (refines ADR-0014 decision 3).** A resolved incident is never reopened. A later event of the same key opens a new incident (`reopened_from` set) only when it is **worse**: a higher score, above the baseline while the incident was not, or a signal the incident did not have; otherwise it is linked to the resolved incident. The next hour is a new key, so a new incident. ADR-0014's "a later scan still sees it" condition has no equivalent for occurrences.
8. **Hourly cap and overflow.** A policy opens at most `DATABASTION_EVENT_INCIDENTS_PER_POLICY_HOUR` new incidents per (policy, agent, target) and database-clock hour (default 50, 1 to 10 000), so noise on one target never affects another. Beyond, the matches of the hour on that target go to one **overflow** incident of the policy and target (no principal), notified once. **Severe events bypass the cap** and open their own incident: a `signature.*` signal, an anomaly (above the principal's baseline), or a score above the overflow incident's highest. A resolved overflow incident follows decision 7.
9. **Fair draining and back-pressure.** `policies.evaluate` evaluates access events first, within **60 %** of its time budget, then the findings (which always run after). Chunks hold at most 200 events and at most 50 per agent, agents interleaved, each agent's events in arrival order through one index range per agent (`access_events_pending_idx`, migration `0025`); no sort of the whole backlog. Evaluation is serialized by an advisory lock. `POST /events` answers `429` + `Retry-After: 30` while the agent has more than **20 000** events not evaluated yet; the agent keeps its batches spooled. An accepted batch wakes the worker; the one-minute schedule catches a lost wake-up.
10. **Notifications.** `incident.opened` payloads carry a `source` discriminator: `finding` or `access_event`. An access-event incident carries the principal, whether it is a fingerprint, the database, the hour and the first event's action, source, rows, score, sensitivity, anomaly flag, signals and objects; never a value. Receivers switch on `source`.

### Refines ADR-0014
- Decision 3 is refined for `access_event` incidents by decisions 6 and 7 above; for findings it is unchanged (with [ADR-0019](0019-incident-reopen-cutoff.md)).
- Decision 1 says that globs are "matched by a linear-time matcher". The matcher (`globMatch`, `console/src/lib/policy-common.ts`) is a greedy two-pointer matcher with one backtrack point and no regular expression: its worst case is **O(n × m)** time for a name of n characters and a pattern of m tokens, O(n + m) space, with no exponential backtracking. Both sides are bounded (names 256 characters by the contract, patterns by `MAX_GLOB_LENGTH`), so one match costs at most about 65 000 steps. The security property ADR-0014 relies on (no catastrophic backtracking on hostile names) holds; "linear-time" is inaccurate.

## Consequences
- The console process cannot erase or rewrite what agents reported, only let it age out after at least 7 days; an event backlog is scored before it can be purged.
- Retention bounds the event table; baselines and replay records are bounded by retention and by the per-target cap. Incidents still have no retention (ADR-0014).
- An incident's event list shows what is left after the retention; its counts stay.
- A policy created after an exfiltration does not raise an incident for it; the events stay visible in the events view until the retention.
- Findings evaluation keeps at least 40 % of each run, and one busy agent cannot delay the others' events; the phase 4 exit criterion (incident in under 2 minutes) relies on the wake-up and on the 2 s worker polling.
- A classifier family exception (ADR-0014 decision 5) cannot hide access events; location exceptions can.
- Webhook receivers written for phase 3 must handle `source: "access_event"` payloads, which have no classifier, location or counts.
- The agent's `429` handling (backoff, spool) covers back-pressure; the contract text does not describe it yet (protocol follow-up, ROADMAP P4-D).

### Residual risks
- **Baseline slow-ramp poisoning.** The capped update limits the effect of one event, not of a principal that raises its volume gradually over many events: each step below the threshold is learnt. Signature, `min_rows`, `min_score` and `min_sensitivity` conditions do not depend on the baseline and remain available.
- **Per-process rate limits.** The `/events` limits (300 requests and 60 stored batches per minute per agent) are in memory, per web process (ROADMAP P4-D). The 20 000 back-pressure threshold is read from the database and holds across processes.
- **Volume is rows only.** Sources that report no `rows` give a score of 0 and feed no baseline; bytes are not used (the contract has no `bytes` field yet; ROADMAP P4-D).
- **Table-level sensitivity.** Sensitivity is per object, not per column: reading only the non-sensitive columns of a table that holds a sensitive column counts as sensitive, and an event object without schema takes the findings of every schema. A name the agent masked as `*` is compared as the literal `*`: only a glob that matches `*` selects it, and it gets the sensitivity of the findings recorded under the same normalized name, if any.
- **Client network grouping.** Hosts that share a /24 or /64 share the coarse principal of their unknown-account and failed-login events: one incident per hour for all of them.

## Rejected alternatives
- **Runtime `DELETE` on `access_events` for retention**: a compromised console process could erase recent evidence. The owner-defined function with a 7-day floor limits it to old, evaluated events.
- **An eviction function taking a number of rows** (first version, migration `0024`): a compromised process could empty a target's baselines. Replaced by one taking the cap (security re-review N4).
- **One incident per event, or per table**: a `pg_dump` would open hundreds of incidents.
- **Dedup per principal with no time bucket**: one long-lived incident per principal would absorb later, unrelated exfiltrations.
- **Keying unknown accounts by their fingerprint or exact address**: random account names or rotating addresses would open one incident each.
- **A cap per policy across all targets**: noise on one target would push another target's incidents into an overflow (security re-review N1).
- **No bypass of the cap**: a flood of low-value matches would hide a dump behind the overflow incident.
- **Reopening the resolved incident, or "resolved suppresses the key until remediated"**: events are occurrences, so there is no later scan to show remediation; a new incident when worse keeps the resolve meaningful without silencing a new exfiltration.
- **Re-evaluating past events on a policy change**: incidents would be raised for accesses hours or weeks old, with notifications, and a policy change would cost a pass over the whole event table.
- **Letting classifier-scoped exceptions cover events through object sensitivity**: a `pii.*` exception would silently remove Audit coverage of every table holding PII.
- **Uncapped baseline update, or a plain mean**: one dump would raise the baseline enough to hide the next one; a plain mean never forgets.
- **A console-generated `volume.*` signal for the baseline verdict**: signals are the agent's (ADR-0007); a separate `anomaly` condition keeps the two apart.
- **A global FIFO over the backlog**: one busy agent would delay every other agent's events.
